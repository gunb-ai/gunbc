## The divergence (from #14608, ruled in lane)

`#14608` had to JOIN `data` initializers onto the `=` line (`java_grammar_structure.dag`, `rust_grammar_structure.dag`) because the v1 host parse route refuses an initializer that begins on the line after the `=` — while the v2 grammar route had ADMITTED the continuation layout in the first place (that is how `#14559` landed it). One text, grammatical to one parser and ungrammatical to the other: `decl_facts`' walk over the v2 grammar and `concept_decl_facts`' fail-closed v1 parse route silently disagree about which files exist.

## Which parser is wrong — decided against the DECLARED grammar, not the corpus

- **v2 declares nothing.** `v2.extdeps.languages.dag` `dag_grammar_data_decl_expr` sequences `data`, ident, `:`, type_expr, `=`, `Expect(dag_production_expr)` with **no `AfterLineBreak` anywhere in it** — while `AfterLineBreak` (evaluated by the matcher via `token_stream_line_break_before`) IS the vocabulary the spec uses when it DOES condition a production on line structure (`dag_grammar_additive_newline_minus_guard`, the dual-role `-`). The continuation acceptance is the undeclared default, not a rule.
- **v1 refuses by accident, and is the odd one out INSIDE v1.** `v1.compiler.parse` `parse_data_after_kw` fed the token right after `ExpectEq` straight into `parse_expr`, which refuses a `Newline` at expression start. Every sibling `=` position in the same parser — `=` of a type alias, of an expression-bodied `fn`, of `let` — calls `skip_newlines` after its `=`. One character, three sibling positions with the line rule and one without, and no grammar declaring either behavior.
- **The corpus cannot decide it** (it has no stake): text-shape census at the fix — **32,393 joined initializers, ZERO authored continuation sites**. The single text-shape match is `src/v1/stage0/tests/fixtures/fact_cardinality_split_brace.dag`, a headerless fragment whose unparseability is the subject of `extract_top_level_decls_captures_split_brace_body` — it stays unparseable after this PR for its own reason (first token `data`, no `module` header), so its digest skip persists unchanged and unentangled.

**Ruling (manager, 2026-10-21):** ONE rule, the permissive one — after the data `=`, the initializer is the expression production with newlines skipped — implemented in v1 by adding the `skip_newlines` call its sibling `=` sites already make. v2 needs no change: its default already IS the rule. Choosing the permissive rule over making v2 strict deletes no expressive capacity the undeclared grammar has always admitted, makes v1 self-consistent, and changes zero accepted text; the strict alternative would only add a declaration nobody's code depends on.

## Fail-closed is NOT weakened

With no initializer at all (`data t: Int =` then a blank line, or the next declaration), an expression cannot start at the `data` keyword — both routes refuse at that token, before and after the fix. That is measured, not argued: the claim's `blank` and `dangling` cells hold on both sides (see Evidence).
