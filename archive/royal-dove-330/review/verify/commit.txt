v2-bootstrap: drive the walk's member route in the reuse controls (PATCH 3, review revision)

Follows sleek-owl-397's and keen-otter-392's source reviews of PATCH 2: the source was
found sound, the controls were not ready. No compile, test, claim, regen, run, dispatch or
push has been performed; nothing added here is labelled RED or GREEN.

Controls
- reading_a_base_changes_the_report_line_and_not_the_published_payload rebuilt on a tally
  whose declared classes cover the refusals it replays (on the classes-free fixture those
  rows are undeclared refusals, the report is not held, and the baseline does not exist),
  and routed through the walk's own sequence standing -> counted -> accepted (judge_read).
- The payload-parity controls that called population_parse_accepted on both sides are gone:
  that function never reads the base, so neither half could fail. They are replaced by
  controls that drive population_parse_member_decided -- the body the walk itself runs --
  over a supplied member: reused-without-the-parser, identity mismatch, unseen digest,
  refused member, parsed member's own line, and a mixed walk in both orders.
- Mutations the controls are named against are listed in the block comment. The real-producer
  inhabitance obligations (the member lookup; the two-walks-of-one-tree byte comparison that
  is the purity oracle; the published artifact) are declared OPEN at the foot of the file,
  not passed.

Source
- population_verbs: population_parse_member's decision split into population_parse_member_decided
  so the claim can supply the member the lookup would return without replacing the decision.
  The caller still performs the lookup; behavior is unchanged.
- compile_cli: the writer's Absent arm is labelled for what it is -- unreachable from any
  accepted invocation, untested on purpose, with its scope named -- and the claim that an empty
  payload "would be read as a complete answer" is removed: membership lookups miss, so an empty
  base grants nothing, which population_verdict_products already says it does not guard.
- parse_verdict_restore: the locator comment states the mechanism (git rev-list --skip=<ordinal>
  names the N-th ancestor strictly below the start; the start itself is reachable only at
  --skip=0, which no caller asks for).
