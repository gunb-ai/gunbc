WHAT ONE MEMBER DOES, from source. population_parse_member (src/v2/cli/population_verbs.dag:486) locates the member in the layout, then population_parse_member_decided (:471) does, in order: module_file_key, content_hash_atom over the member's full text, population_parse_standing (:415), population_parse_counted (:438), and only when standing is not Reused, acquisition_parse (src/v2/compiler/module_acquisition.dag:909) which is tokenize_prepared against the SHARED prepared lexer env.lex, then parse_module_prepared against the SHARED prepared grammar env.grammar (bind_outcome at :872-877). The parse tree is discarded - the verdict carries the cause and never the tree (the MemberParse type comment at :892-896). On refusal only, acquisition_refusal_at runs line_starts over the content. So the lexer and grammar are built once per walk, not per member: there is no per-member front-end rebuild to remove.

WHAT THAT MEANS, and what it does not let me claim.
1. There is no duplicated work inside the walk worth attacking. The front end is shared, the tree is dropped, and the fixed machinery is already under 1%. The only cross-step overlap I can see is that the walk re-tokenizes modules the emit steps' compile transaction also tokenized (pool_source_tokenize, 14.3 s recorded) - about 1% of one walk, not worth a change.
2. The per-member parse is the required semantics, not overhead: the population must be shown to parse.
