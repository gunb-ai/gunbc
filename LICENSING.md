# Licensing — plain language

gunbc is released under the [Business Source License 1.1](LICENSE) with an
Additional Use Grant. This page is a plain-language summary; the [LICENSE](LICENSE)
file is the agreement and controls wherever the two differ.

## The short version

- **Anything you build with gunbc is yours.** Source, binaries, packages,
  documents, reports, hardware descriptions, deployed services — none of it
  inherits this license, and you owe nothing for it. Where gunbc deliberately
  embeds our own runtime or support code into what it produces, that embedded
  code is MIT.
- **Non-production use is free for everyone**, at any size: evaluating,
  developing, testing, researching, teaching, tinkering.
- **Production use is free** unless your corporate group's consolidated revenue
  for its last completed fiscal year was over **USD 100 million**. Above that,
  talk to us about a commercial license.
- **Nonprofits, schools, and public-sector bodies** acting in their public
  capacity are free regardless of size.
- **Every release becomes MIT two years after it is published.** The clock runs
  per release, automatically.

There is no registration, no telemetry, no phone-home, no seat count, no audit
clause, no royalty on output, and no obligation to contribute anything back.

## Is this open source?

Not yet. Inside its two-year window a release is **source-available**: you can
read, modify, redistribute and use it under the terms above, but the Open Source
Initiative would not call it open source. Two years after it ships, that release
is plain MIT — open source, forever.

## Why this shape

The paid boundary is the distance between the current frontier and the free
trailing edge, which exists only while the project is actively developed. If
development stops, the trailing edge catches up and the commercial boundary
disappears on its own — no adjudication. The clock decides.

## Do I owe anything?

Work through it in order:

1. Is your use non-production (evaluation, development, testing, research,
   teaching, personal)? → **No.**
2. Are you a nonprofit, educational institution, or public-sector body acting
   in that capacity? → **No.**
3. Was your corporate group's consolidated revenue for its last completed
   fiscal year at or below USD 100 million? → **No.**
4. Otherwise → production use needs a commercial license. Get in touch.

"Corporate group" means the entities consolidated in your group's own financial
statements — the accounting definition your auditor already applies. A
contractor running gunbc on your behalf is your use. Consuming someone else's
product or output built with gunbc is not your use at all.
